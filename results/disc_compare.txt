Layered sweep against L-sweeps on a constant-contrast disc, with the Mie series as
the reference.  The disc has radius R = 1/4 centred at (1/2, 1/2) in the unit
square -- the unit disc after rescaling lengths by 4, so its size parameter is
omega R = omega/4 -- velocity c_in = 0.8 inside (index 1.25, m = -0.5625), 1 outside;
incident field the package's default plane wave e^{i omega x2}; h = lambda/8, b = 8,
C = 12, GMRES(20) to 1e-6.  One process per row on a 16-core Ryzen 7950X3D
(examples/disc_compare.jl).  L-sweeps: boxes of ~64 points, compact factors; below
omega/2pi = 16 that is a single box, i.e. the exact solve of H in single precision.

  julia --project=. -t 16 examples/disc_compare.jl 2 4 8 16 32 64 128 256
  julia --project=. -t 16 examples/disc_compare.jl 256 --only=direct

The package solves for the scattered field, u_sc + omega^2 K[m u_sc] = -omega^2 K[m u_inc],
so the Mie error is |u_sc - (u_Mie - e^{i omega x2})| / |u_Mie - e^{i omega x2}| in the
l2 norm over 20 000 random grid points (all points when n <= 127).  Every row at a
frequency solves the same discrete system to 1e-6, so the Mie error is the same on
every row: it is the discretisation error, and it validates the solution rather than
the preconditioner.

COLUMNS.  Every one is measured.
  omega/2pi  frequency; the grid is n = 8(omega/2pi) - 1 at eight points per
             wavelength, and the disc is omega/(4 pi) wavelengths across
  n, CDD     grid points per side, and the L-sweeps box decomposition
  inner solver  the approximate inverse of H the preconditioner uses; "inner 6"
             runs up to six GMRES iterations on H per application, to 1e-3, in
             place of a single sweep
  setup      building the preconditioner: sparsification, H, the factorisation
  apply      one preconditioner application, timed once: S r, the inner solve,
             and the scatter back to the physical points
  outer      iterations of the outer Krylov solver on the dense system
  sweeps     preconditioner applications performed inside that solve; LU solves
             on the exact-H row
  fwd        forward maps: applications of A = I + omega^2 K[m .], each an FFT
             convolution on the padded grid
  Hmv        applications -- not solves -- of the sparsified operator H, one per
             inner GMRES iteration; only the two-level rows perform any
  t_sweep    the cost of one sweep: `apply` divided by the sweeps that
             application performs, one for the single-level rows and eight for
             the two-level rows from omega/2pi = 16 up.  It carries S r and the
             scatter with it, 0.050s of 1.100 at n = 2047
  t_fwd      the cost of one forward map
  t_Hmv      the cost of one matvec with H.  This and t_fwd depend on the grid
             alone -- not on the medium, not on the preconditioner -- so one
             measurement per size serves every row (disc_compare.jl
             --costs-only); t_Hmv is already inside the two-level rows' t_sweep
  solve      the outer Krylov solve, setup excluded
  relres     true relative residual ||A u - b|| / ||b|| after the solve
  peak       peak resident set of the process

THE COUNTS follow the solvers' schedule.  A left-preconditioned GMRES(20) applies
operator and preconditioner once per iteration and once more at every restart,
plus one preconditioner application on the initial residual, so the single-level
rows have fwd = outer + r and sweeps = outer + 1 + r with r = floor((outer-1)/20)
restarts, and the two-level rows have Hmv = sweeps - outer.  Only the two-level
sweep counts cannot be derived: each application runs an inner GMRES that stops
at 1e-3 or its cap of six, two sweeps per application at omega/2pi <= 8 and eight
from 16 upward.  At n = 2047 a forward map costs 6.6 times an H matvec, 0.308s
against 0.046s, and that ratio is the whole reason the two-level scheme pays.

READING THE ITERATIONS.  The exact solve of H needs 3, 4, 5, 10 iterations at
omega/2pi = 32..256: the sparsification's own floor is modest, and only about twice
the lens's 3.  Everything above it is the inexactness of the sweeps on a medium
that reflects.  The lens is smooth and reflects weakly, so one sweep pass --
layered or L-shaped -- captures almost the whole field and the counts stay at 9
and 14 at 256; the disc's interface sends waves back, and each pass captures one
more reflection, so the layered sweep needs 156 and the L-sweep 328.  It is not
the absorber: a three-wavelength pad takes the L-sweep count only from 328 to 297
while doubling the application cost, where on the lens it took 14 to 8.  The
restarts are a real cost at these counts: 328 iterations of GMRES(20) carry
sixteen restarts, each an extra forward map and an extra sweep.

WHAT THE WORK COLUMNS SHOW.  The two-level scheme wins at 256 without doing fewer
sweeps -- it does 176 against the layered sweep's 164 -- but its sweeps are
L-sweeps at about 0.96s each rather than layered sweeps at 2.08s, and it cuts the
forward maps from 163 to 24.  Against single-level L-sweeps the gain is plainer
still: 176 sweeps against 345 and 24 forward maps against 344.  What the inner
iterations buy is that most of the work moves onto the sparse system H, where an
iteration costs a sweep and a sparse matvec, and off the dense system, where an
iteration costs a forward map plus Krylov orthogonalisation against a basis of
4.2-million-entry vectors.  The exact-H row is the limit of that trade: ten
forward maps, eleven LU solves, 18s.

ACCOUNTING.  sweeps x t_sweep + fwd x t_fwd should be most of the solve time, the
rest being the Krylov orthogonalisation.  At omega/2pi = 256 it accounts for 392s
of the layered sweep's 403s, 176s of the two-level scheme's 178s and 17.0s of the
exact solve's 18.3s -- within a few percent.  For the single-level L-sweeps rows
it overshoots, 486s against 458s at pad 8 and 619s against 567s at pad 16, which
says the single timed application those rows report is about 8 percent pessimistic
relative to the same sweep run back to back inside the solve; the counts and the
solve times are unaffected.
