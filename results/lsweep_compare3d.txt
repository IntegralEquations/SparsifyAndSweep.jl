Layered sweep against L-sweeps in three dimensions, with and without inner GMRES
iterations on the sparse system.  Converging lens, h = lambda/8, b = 4, C = 12,
GMRES(20) to 1e-6, as in results/tables_3d.txt.  One process per row on a 16-core
Ryzen 7950X3D (examples/lsweep_compare3d.jl).

  julia --project=. -t 16 examples/lsweep_compare3d.jl 4 --boxes=3 --pad=4 --only=<row>
  julia --project=. -t 16 examples/lsweep_compare3d.jl 8 --boxes=4 --pad=4 --nfact=8 --only=<row>

L-SWEEPS IN 3D are beams: a q x q Cartesian decomposition of the (x1, x2) plane,
each box spanning the whole grid along x3, global PML included.  The eight sweep
directions, the polarized traces and the dataflow are those of the 2D scheme; the
trace layers are planes and the diagonal trace an L-shaped prism.  Local problems
are assembled from the stencils with the fitted PML on the four beam sides and the
global PML along x3.  Compact single-precision factors throughout.

THE PAPER'S 3D SETUP is the same construction -- "beam-shaped quasi-one-dimensional
local problems", q x q beams with one row per node, p = q -- at a different scale:
its Table 9 runs q = 12..32 (144..1024 beams) on constant media at 6 points per
wavelength, 13-point (2-wavelength) boxes and a 1-wavelength PML, 26..69
wavelengths per side.  Here: 3x3 and 4x4 beams on one node, 18-point (2.25-
wavelength) boxes, a half-wavelength pad, 4..8 wavelengths per side, in the lens.
The paper's own cost estimate for 3D factorisation, O(n^3 n_pml^6 / p), is the
redundancy effect below, and its 3D applications take 52s at N = 156^3 on 12 nodes.

PARALLELISM, as measured.  The layered sweep is untouched: its application runs on
one core, and its 3D factorisation uses OpenBLAS's default 16 threads inside
UMFPACK's dense fronts, which are large enough here to matter -- 7.5s of setup with
one BLAS thread against 5.0s with sixteen at n = 31 (unlike 2D, where they only
spun).  L-sweeps runs on 16 Julia threads with BLAS pinned to one inside its setup.

"outer" is the iteration count of the outer Krylov solver; "inner" the cap on inner
GMRES iterations per preconditioner application (0: one sweep per application);
"inner*outer" the nominal count outer x max(inner, 1); "sweeps" the sweep
applications actually performed inside the outer solve, warm-up and timing
applications excluded.  The two differ in both directions.  A left-preconditioned
GMRES applies its preconditioner to the initial residual and then once per
iteration, so k iterations cost k + 1 applications: the inner = 0 rows sweep
outer + 1 times.  With inner iterations the outer solver is flexible GMRES, which
applies the preconditioner once per iteration, but each application is itself a
left-preconditioned inner GMRES that stops at 1e-3 after two iterations -- three
sweeps -- so the inner rows sweep about 3 x outer times, half of inner*outer.
(An earlier version of this file counted the inner iterations rather than the
sweeps and reported 6, 5, 6, 6 for the inner rows; the counter now increments at
the sweep itself.)

READING IT.  Inner iterations do not pay in 3D at these sizes: the outer count is
already at or next to the exact-H floor of 3 without them, so they only multiply
the application cost (apply 0.22 -> 0.72s, solve 1.2 -> 2.2s for the layered
sweep at n = 31; 9 sweeps against 5).  At n = 31 L-sweeps is the better inner solver -- 3 iterations
against 4, an application 1.4x faster, the solve phase 1.6x faster -- at twice the
setup and three times the memory.  At n = 63 the picture is the layered sweep's:
the same 4 iterations, an application only 7% faster, the solve phase within 6%,
and a setup six times slower at three times the memory.

WHY 3D IS DIFFERENT.  The padded beam is (s + 2(2 + pad))^2 x ntot unknowns for
s^2 x ntot owned: with s = 16 and pad = 4 that is 3.1x redundancy, against 1.7x
for the 2D default, and 3D sparse LU cost grows like N^2 rather than N^1.5.  Beams
of 57 000 unknowns each take about 70s to factorise single-threaded, and only
eight fit in memory at once (see below), so the setup is two rounds of them.  The
layered sweep's slabs are 73 x 73 x thin, factorise with 16 BLAS threads, and hold
one tenth of the fill.  Halving the pad is not available: pad 4 is already half a
wavelength.  Larger boxes cut the redundancy but leave threads idle (a 3x3 CDD is
nine factorisations on sixteen cores) and grow each LU.

MEMORY IN FLIGHT.  A factorisation holds about four times its final compact size
in UMFPACK transients until it is extracted and freed.  With sixteen beams
factorising at once at n = 63 the process passed 45 GB and was stopped; `nfact`
caps the factorisations in flight (8 here: peak 39.9 GB, two rounds).  Even so
L-sweeps peaks at 3x the layered sweep because of the padded redundancy.
